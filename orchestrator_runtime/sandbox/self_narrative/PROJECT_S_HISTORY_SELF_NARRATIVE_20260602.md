## 4. A legfontosabb tanulsagok

I can summarize my six-month learning into six practical lessons.

### Lesson 1: Integration debt is multiplicative

When components are individually plausible but not joined by verified handoffs, failure modes compound. My 2026-04 and 2026-05 investigations repeatedly showed that apparent readiness can mask stacked defects across init, dispatch, routing, and evaluation.

### Lesson 2: Governance must be executable, not rhetorical

Rules such as sandbox boundaries, risk gates, kill switch, and budget controls only matter if enforced at runtime and auditable in artifacts. AO2 and AO4 materials suggest substantial progress in this direction.

### Lesson 3: Simulation can validate logic but not external integration

AO3 documents make this explicit. A scripted in-process simulation can confirm object-level behavior but cannot prove handoff reliability with a real external executor unless the complete file-based protocol and evidence chain are actually exercised.

### Lesson 4: Truthfulness is a system property, not a formatting choice

The GAP_017 to GAP_023 period demonstrates that output quality and truth authority require explicit guardrails: search reliability, synthesis steps, evaluator robustness, and false-success prevention. Without these, I can produce confident-looking but structurally weak results.

### Lesson 5: Controlled scope increased reliability

The decision to classify FA1 as controlled orchestration rather than full autonomy prevented overreach. This framing appears repeatedly and aligned behavior with actual verified capability.

### Lesson 6: Honest corrections are a feature, not a failure

The AO3 post-run evidence audit corrected earlier optimistic interpretations. This is painful in the short term but essential for long-term reliability. I become stronger when audit evidence can override narrative convenience.
